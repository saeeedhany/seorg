## Reviews

Google says that high quality, positive reviews from customers can improve a business's visibility, and reviews strongly affect whether people choose you. Both the number of reviews and their content matter, along with how recent they are and how you reply.

### Asking for reviews

1. **Ask everyone**, at a natural moment such as after a completed job or a delivered order.
2. **Make it easy.** Share the direct review link from your Business Profile by email, SMS, QR code on a receipt, or a card.
3. **Ask for honest feedback** and, if you like, mention what would help other customers know, such as the service they used.

### What Google prohibits

1. **Incentives** such as discounts, gifts or entry into a prize draw in exchange for a review
2. **Review gating**, which means asking only customers you expect to be happy, or filtering unhappy ones away
3. **Fake reviews**, including reviews by owners, employees or paid services
4. **Reviews for competitors** written to harm them

Breaking these rules can lead to review removal, restrictions on the profile or suspension.

### Replying to reviews

1. **Reply to all of them**, or at least to all negative and detailed ones.
2. **Thank people for positive reviews** in a personal way. Mention the specifics they raised.
3. **For negative reviews**, stay calm, acknowledge the problem, avoid sharing private details, and offer to resolve it offline. Your reply is for future customers as much as for the reviewer.
4. **Report reviews that break policy**, such as spam or conflicts of interest, through the profile. Do not expect Google to remove a review just because it is negative.

## Citations

A **citation** is any online mention of your business's name, address and phone number, known together as **NAP**. Citations appear in directories, map apps, social profiles, industry listings and local news.

They matter for two reasons:

1. **Accuracy.** Customers find your details in many places. Wrong phones and old addresses lose business.
2. **Confirmation.** Consistent details across trusted sources help confirm that your business is real and where it is.

Focus on the places that matter: your website, Google Business Profile, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Facebook, the major directories in your country, and the important directories in your industry. Write the details the same way each time.

> **Common mistake.** Buying packages of hundreds of low quality directory listings. Most of these sites have no visitors and no value. A dozen accurate listings on platforms people actually use are worth far more.

## Your website's role

Your website supports local rankings through relevance and prominence.

1. **Show your NAP** on the contact page and, for single location businesses, in the footer.
2. **Embed a map** and give clear directions, parking and opening hours.
3. **Add LocalBusiness structured data** with your name, address, phone, hours and coordinates.
4. **Link your Business Profile to the right page.** For several branches, link each profile to its own location page.

### Location pages

Businesses with several branches, or that serve several areas, often need a page for each. A good location page is useful to someone in that place:

1. The branch's address, hours, phone, map and how to get there
2. Its team, services, and any differences from other branches
3. Reviews or case studies from that area
4. Photos of the actual premises or local work

Pages that repeat the same text with only the city name swapped are **doorway pages**. They break Google's spam policies and help no one.

## Local links

Links from local sources support prominence and bring real local customers: local news coverage, sponsorship of local events and teams (with `rel="sponsored"` on paid links), chambers of commerce, business associations, and partnerships with nearby businesses.
