## What a keyword is

A **keyword** is a search query: the words someone types or says into a search engine. Keyword research answers three questions:

1. What do the people you want to reach search for?
2. Which of those searches can your site realistically serve well?
3. Which page should serve each one?

## Where to find ideas

**Your own Search Console data.** The Performance report shows queries you already appear for. Look for queries with many impressions but a low position. These are the fastest wins.

**Google itself.** Autocomplete suggestions, People also ask questions and the related searches at the bottom of results all show real search language.

**Your customers.** Use the words from support emails, sales calls, reviews and site search logs. People often describe problems very differently from how businesses describe products.

**Communities.** Reddit, forums, Quora and comment sections show the questions people ask and the words they use.

**Keyword tools.** Google Keyword Planner is free with a Google Ads account, but without active ad spend it shows broad volume ranges. Ahrefs, Semrush and similar paid tools show estimated volumes, difficulty, and the keywords competitors rank for.

**Google Trends** shows relative interest over time. Use it to spot seasonality and rising or falling topics.

## Judging a keyword

Do not choose keywords by volume alone. Weigh four things.

**Intent.** What does the searcher want, and can your page provide it? A software company cannot rank a product page for "what is project management", because searchers want an explanation. The [next lesson]({{ '/learn/search-intent/' | relative_url }}) covers this in depth.

**Business value.** Would ranking for this bring people who might become customers, subscribers or readers you care about? A small number of high value searches beats a large number of irrelevant ones.

**Difficulty.** Search the query and look at who ranks. If every result is a famous brand with thousands of linking sites, a new site will struggle. Tool difficulty scores are a rough estimate of this, based mainly on links.

**Volume.** How many people search for it. Treat tool numbers as estimates: they are modeled, rounded, and often wrong for small or new queries.

> **Long tail searches.** Specific, longer queries such as "waterproof trail running shoes for women wide fit" have low volume each, but most searches are like this. They are easier to rank for, and the searcher often knows exactly what they want.

## Grouping keywords into topics

Many keywords are just different ways of asking the same thing. Google understands this, and it ranks the same page for all of them.

So group keywords by **intent**, not by exact wording:

1. Search two similar keywords. If mostly the same pages rank for both, they belong to one group and one page.
2. If the results are very different, the intents differ and need separate pages.

**Keyword cannibalization** is the problem that grouping prevents: several of your own pages targeting the same intent, so none of them is clearly the best. It only applies to pages that serve the *same intent*. Two pages about related but different questions are fine.

## A keyword map

A keyword map assigns each topic group to one URL. It keeps a site organized as it grows.

1. **/guides/repot-snake-plant/** targets "how to repot a snake plant", "snake plant repotting" and "when to repot snake plant". The intent is to learn how to do it.
2. **/plants/snake-plant/** targets "buy snake plant" and "snake plant for sale". The intent is to purchase.
3. **/guides/snake-plant-care/** targets "snake plant care", "how often to water snake plant" and "snake plant light needs". The intent is to learn about care.

Before writing a new page, check the map. If an existing page already serves the intent, improve that page instead.

> **Common mistake.** Creating a separate page for every keyword variation, such as "best CRM for small business", "best small business CRM" and "top CRM small companies". This produces thin, competing pages. One strong page serves all three.
