## Start with something worth linking to

Outreach for an ordinary page is hard work with poor results. Outreach for something genuinely useful is much easier. Before you ask for links, ask what on your site deserves them.

Assets that tend to earn links:

1. **Original data.** Surveys, studies and analysis of data you hold. Journalists and bloggers need facts to cite.
2. **Tools and calculators.** A free mortgage calculator or image compressor gets linked from resource pages for years.
3. **The definitive guide.** The clearest, most complete explanation of a topic in your field, kept up to date.
4. **Visual explainers.** Diagrams, maps and charts that make a complex idea simple. Let people embed them with a credit link.
5. **Expert opinion.** A distinctive, well argued view from someone who knows the field.

## Digital PR

Digital PR means getting coverage, and the links that come with it, from news sites and publications. It works by giving journalists what they need:

1. **Data led stories.** Run a survey or analyze data, then pitch the most newsworthy finding to relevant reporters.
2. **Reactive comment.** When news breaks in your field, offer a short, quotable expert view to reporters covering it quickly.
3. **Journalist requests.** Services where reporters ask for expert sources. Answer quickly and only where you are a genuine expert.

Coverage builds brand awareness as well as links, which is valuable in its own right.

## Other reliable methods

**Unlinked mentions.** Search for your brand, products and people. Where a page mentions you without a link, ask kindly for one.

**Broken link building.** Find pages in your field that link to dead pages. Offer your own resource as a replacement if it truly covers the same thing.

**Resource pages.** Many organizations keep lists of useful resources for their audience, such as universities, associations and libraries. If your page belongs on one, ask.

**Partners and suppliers.** Businesses you work with may list you as a stockist, partner or case study.

**Competitor research.** Use a backlink tool to see who links to competitors. Sites that link to several of them but not to you may be interested, and the pattern shows which types of content earn links in your field.

**Guest contributions.** Writing for respected publications in your field builds reputation and reaches new readers. Do it for the audience. Google's spam policies cover large scale guest posting campaigns built around keyword rich links.

## Outreach that gets replies

1. **Choose recipients carefully.** One relevant person beats a hundred random addresses.
2. **Show you know their work.** Mention something specific they wrote.
3. **Explain the value to them or their readers**, not to you.
4. **Keep it short**, a few sentences with one clear request.
5. **Follow up once**, then stop.

> **Example.** "Hi Sara, I enjoyed your guide to balcony gardening, especially the section on wind. You link to a soil pH chart from the university extension office that now returns an error. We keep an updated chart with the same data here, in case it is useful as a replacement: [link]. Either way, thanks for the guide."

## Measuring results

Track the number of new **referring domains** (unique sites linking to you) rather than total links, and note whether they are relevant. Also watch for the outcomes that matter: rankings and traffic for the pages you are building links to, and referral visits from the links themselves.
