## Anatomy of an organic result

Each standard organic result has the same parts:

1. **Site name and favicon.** Google takes these from your site, and you can influence them with structured data and a site icon.
2. **URL or breadcrumb.** This shows where the page lives on the site.
3. **Title link.** This is the clickable headline. Google usually builds it from the page's `<title>` element, but it may use a heading or other text if the title is missing, too long, or a poor match for the page.
4. **Snippet.** This is the description below. Google often uses the meta description, but it frequently writes its own from page text that better matches the query.

Some results also show extras such as review stars, prices, dates or images. These are **rich results**. They usually come from structured data, which is the subject of [its own lesson]({{ '/learn/structured-data/' | relative_url }}).

## Search features

Google adds many other elements to results pages. Which ones appear depends on the query.

**Ads** appear above and below organic results and are labeled "Sponsored".

**Featured snippet.** This is a short answer taken from one page and shown above or among the top results, with a link. Pages that answer a question directly and clearly, near the top of the page, are more likely to be chosen.

**People also ask.** These are expandable questions related to the search, each answered with a snippet from a page. They are a good source of questions to cover in your content.

**Local pack.** This is a map with a short list of businesses, shown for searches with local intent such as "dentist near me". It comes from Google Business Profiles, covered in [the local module]({{ '/learn/local-ranking/' | relative_url }}).

**Knowledge panel.** This is a box of facts about a well known entity such as a company, person or place.

**Images, videos, top stories, shopping results, and discussions and forums.** These are blocks of specific content types. Each has its own eligibility rules.

## AI Overviews and AI Mode

**AI Overviews** are summaries generated by Google that appear at the top of some results pages, with links to the pages they draw on. They launched in the United States in May 2024 and have since expanded to many countries and languages. **AI Mode**, launched in the US in May 2025, is a separate conversational search experience built on the same idea.

Google's guidance on these features is short: there is no special markup or file needed. A page is eligible to be shown as a supporting link if it is indexed and eligible to appear with a snippet in normal search. The same practices that help in regular search apply here.

Traffic from these features is counted inside the normal Web search totals in Search Console. They are not reported separately.

> **Be careful with claims about "AI optimization".** Many new services promise special techniques for AI search. Check every claim against Google's own documentation. Most of the reliable advice is the same as in the rest of this course: be crawlable, be clear, and be the best answer.

## Zero click searches

Many searches now end without a click to any website. The answer is shown on the results page itself, in a featured snippet, a knowledge panel, a calculator, a weather box or an AI Overview. A 2024 clickstream study by SparkToro estimated that close to 60 percent of Google searches in the US and EU ended without a click to the open web.

This changes how you measure success:

1. **Impressions matter.** Being seen for the right queries builds recognition even without a click.
2. **Choose queries with care.** Simple factual questions rarely send traffic. Queries that need depth, comparison, tools or trust still do.
3. **Compare like with like.** A falling click through rate can mean the results page changed, not that your page got worse.

## Look before you write

Before you target any query, search for it yourself, ideally in a private window. Note which features appear and what kind of pages rank. The results page tells you what Google thinks searchers want, which is the core of the [search intent lesson]({{ '/learn/search-intent/' | relative_url }}).
