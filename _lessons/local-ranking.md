## Local search results

When a search has local intent, such as "dentist near me", "bakery in Alexandria" or just "pharmacy", Google often shows a **local pack**: a map with a short list of businesses. Selecting "more places" opens Google Maps style results. These listings come from **Google Business Profiles**, not from ordinary web pages.

Local SEO matters to businesses that serve customers in person: shops, restaurants, clinics, trades, and service businesses that visit customers.

## The three local ranking factors

Google states that local results are based mainly on three things.

**Relevance** is how well a profile matches the search. It comes from your categories, the services and products you list, your description, and your website.

**Distance** is how far the business is from the searcher's location, or from a location named in the search. You cannot change it.

**Prominence** is how well known the business is. Google uses information from across the web: links, articles, directories, and the number and quality of reviews. Prominence in the real world counts too, so well known landmarks and brands tend to rank higher. Organic web rankings also play a part.

## Setting up your Business Profile

Create or claim your profile at google.com/business and complete verification. Then work through each section.

1. **Business name.** Use your real name, exactly as it appears on your signage, website and documents. No extra keywords or locations.
2. **Primary category.** Choose the category that describes your core business as specifically as possible, such as "Pediatric dentist" rather than "Dentist" if that is what you are. This is the most important single choice in the profile.
3. **Additional categories.** Add only those that describe other real parts of the business.
4. **Address or service area.** Show your address if customers visit you. If you travel to customers and do not serve them at your address, hide the address and list the areas you serve instead.
5. **Phone and website.** Use a local number if you have one, and link to the most relevant page, such as the location page for a business with several branches.
6. **Hours.** Keep regular hours accurate and add special hours for holidays. Wrong hours frustrate customers and lead to bad reviews.
7. **Services or products.** List them with clear names and descriptions. They support relevance.
8. **Attributes.** Add accessibility, payment options and other details that apply.
9. **Description.** Write a plain summary of what you do and who you serve. Do not stuff keywords or add links.
10. **Photos.** Add real photos of the premises, team, products and work. Keep them current.

## Keeping the profile healthy

1. **Keep information accurate** whenever anything changes. Google and the public can suggest edits, so check your profile regularly.
2. **Answer questions and reply to reviews.** The [next lesson]({{ '/learn/reviews-and-citations/' | relative_url }}) covers reviews.
3. **Posts** (updates, offers, events) can inform customers who view your profile. They are optional and are not a major ranking factor.

> **Common mistake.** Using a virtual office or a mailbox service address to appear in a city where the business has no real presence. Google's guidelines require a real location staffed during business hours, and such profiles are often suspended.

## Suspensions

Profiles that break the guidelines can be suspended or removed. Common causes include keyword stuffed names, fake addresses, multiple profiles for one location, and incorrect categories. If a profile is suspended, fix the cause and request reinstatement through Google's support process, with evidence such as photos of signage and business documents.
