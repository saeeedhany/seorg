## The short definition

Search engine optimization (SEO) is the work of making your pages easy for search engines to find, understand and trust, so they show those pages to people who are searching for what you offer.

When you search on Google you see two kinds of results. **Paid results** are ads. They carry a "Sponsored" label and appear because an advertiser paid for the click. **Organic results** are everything Google ranks on its own judgment. SEO is about the organic results. There is no way to pay Google for a better organic position.

## What SEO is not

**It is not a set of tricks.** Hiding keywords in white text, buying links and publishing thousands of near identical pages are all covered by Google's spam policies. They can get a site demoted or removed from results. The methods in this course are the ones Google documents and supports.

**It is not a one time task.** Competitors publish, Google updates its systems, and your own site changes. SEO is ongoing maintenance, like keeping a shop clean and stocked.

**It is not guaranteed.** Google decides what ranks. Anyone who promises a specific position is promising something they do not control.

**It is not fast.** Google's own page on hiring an SEO says it usually takes four months to a year before improvements show a benefit. New sites take longer than established ones.

## The areas of SEO

Every SEO problem belongs to at least one of these areas. You will study each one in its own module.

**Technical SEO** makes sure search engines can crawl your pages, render them, and add them to their index. If this fails, nothing else matters, because Google never sees the page properly.

**Content** is about matching what people search for. It covers keyword research, understanding what the searcher wants, and writing pages that serve them well.

**Links and authority** cover how other websites point to yours. Links help Google discover pages and judge how much the wider web trusts them.

**Local SEO** applies to businesses that serve people in a specific place. It focuses on map results and your Google Business Profile.

**Measurement** ties it together. Without data you cannot tell whether a change helped, hurt, or did nothing.

## Why the course runs in this order

The modules follow the order in which problems block each other:

1. You need to know how search works before you can diagnose anything.
2. A page that cannot be indexed cannot rank, so technical foundations come before content.
3. A page that does not match what searchers want will not hold rankings, however many links point to it, so content comes before links.
4. Local SEO builds on all three.
5. Measurement comes last because you need the rest of the vocabulary to read the data.

You can jump to any lesson, but if you are new, read them in order. Each lesson assumes you know the ones before it.

## Sources you can trust

SEO advice varies a lot in quality. Use this order of trust:

1. **Google Search Central documentation** at developers.google.com/search. This is how Google describes its own systems.
2. **Google's Search Status Dashboard and Search Central blog** for confirmed updates and changes.
3. **The Search Quality Rater Guidelines.** This is the handbook Google gives the people who rate search results to test changes. It explains what Google considers high quality, but raters do not set any individual site's rankings.
4. **Well designed studies from SEO tool companies.** These are useful, but they show correlation, not cause. A study that finds "top pages have more words" does not prove that adding words improves rankings.

> **Watch out for tool scores.** Metrics such as Domain Authority (Moz) or Domain Rating (Ahrefs) are estimates made by those companies. Google does not use them. They are handy for comparing sites, but improving them is never the goal in itself.
